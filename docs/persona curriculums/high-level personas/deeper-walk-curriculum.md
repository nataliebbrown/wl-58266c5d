# Deeper Walk Curriculum

A comprehensive program for established believers seeking to deepen their relationship with Christ, expand their biblical knowledge, and grow in spiritual maturity. This curriculum bridges the gap between foundational discipleship and formal seminary training.

---

## Track One: Intimate Knowledge of God

### The Nature and Character of God

- **The Attributes of God** – Systematic study of who God is: holiness, sovereignty, omniscience, love, justice, mercy, and more
- **The Names of God** – Hebrew and Greek names revealing God's character throughout Scripture
- **The Holiness of God** – Understanding God's transcendence and our response
- **The Sovereignty of God** – Divine providence, human responsibility, and trusting God's plan
- **The Love of God** – The breadth, depth, and implications of divine love
- **The Wrath of God** – A neglected attribute and its place in biblical theology
- **God as Creator and Sustainer** – Theology of creation and ongoing providence

### Deepening Your Life with Christ

- **The Person of Christ** – Fully God and fully man: the hypostatic union explored
- **The Offices of Christ** – Prophet, Priest, and King
- **Christ in the Old Testament** – Typology, prophecy, and Christophanies
- **The Atonement: Theories and Theology** – How Christ's death saves: ransom, substitution, satisfaction, and more
- **Union with Christ** – The believer's mystical and vital connection to Jesus
- **Abiding in Christ** – Moving from theology to lived experience (John 15)
- **The Supremacy of Christ** – Jesus as Lord over all creation and every area of life

### Life in the Spirit

- **The Person of the Holy Spirit** – Identity, deity, and personhood
- **The Work of the Holy Spirit** – Conviction, regeneration, sanctification, empowerment
- **Walking in the Spirit** – Daily dependence and sensitivity to the Spirit's leading
- **The Gifts of the Spirit** – Understanding, discovering, and exercising spiritual gifts
- **The Fruit of the Spirit: In-Depth** – Character formation through the Spirit's work
- **The Spirit and Prayer** – Praying in and through the Holy Spirit
- **Discerning the Spirit's Voice** – Guidance, wisdom, and avoiding deception

## Track Two: Mastering the Scriptures

### Old Testament Studies

#### Pentateuch (Torah)
- **Genesis: Creation, Fall, and Promise** – Foundations of biblical theology
- **Exodus: Redemption and Covenant** – Deliverance, law, and tabernacle
- **Leviticus: Holiness and Sacrifice** – The sacrificial system and its fulfillment in Christ
- **Numbers: Wilderness Wanderings** – Faith, failure, and God's faithfulness
- **Deuteronomy: Covenant Renewal** – Moses' final sermons and the heart of the law

#### Historical Books
- **Joshua and Judges** – Conquest, cycles, and the need for a king
- **Ruth and Esther** – Providence, redemption, and women in God's story
- **1 & 2 Samuel** – The rise of kingship and the heart of David
- **1 & 2 Kings** – The divided kingdom and prophetic witness
- **1 & 2 Chronicles** – Retelling history with theological purpose
- **Ezra, Nehemiah, and the Return** – Restoration, reform, and rebuilding

#### Wisdom Literature
- **Job: Suffering and the Sovereignty of God** – Wrestling with theodicy
- **Psalms: The Prayer Book of the Bible** – Genres, theology, and devotional use
- **Proverbs: The Art of Skillful Living** – Wisdom for everyday decisions
- **Ecclesiastes: Meaning Under the Sun** – Vanity, purpose, and fearing God
- **Song of Solomon: Love, Marriage, and Divine Romance** – Interpretation and application

#### Prophetic Books
- **Isaiah: The Gospel Prophet** – Judgment, comfort, and the Servant Songs
- **Jeremiah and Lamentations** – The weeping prophet and covenant faithfulness
- **Ezekiel: Visions of God's Glory** – Judgment, restoration, and the new temple
- **Daniel: Faithfulness in Exile** – Apocalyptic literature and kingdom hope
- **The Twelve Minor Prophets** – Message and relevance of each prophet

#### Old Testament Theology
- **Covenant Theology** – The unifying thread of Scripture from Adam to Christ
- **The Messianic Hope** – Tracing the promise of the coming King
- **Old Testament Ethics** – Applying ancient law to modern life
- **Typology and the Old Testament** – Shadows pointing to Christ

### New Testament Studies

#### The Gospels
- **Matthew: The King and His Kingdom** – Jesus as Messiah to Israel
- **Mark: The Suffering Servant** – Action, discipleship, and the cross
- **Luke: The Savior for All People** – Compassion, outcasts, and the Spirit
- **John: The Word Made Flesh** – Signs, discourses, and believing
- **Harmony of the Gospels** – Integrating the four accounts
- **The Parables of Jesus: In-Depth** – Interpretation and kingdom implications
- **The Miracles of Jesus** – Meaning, purpose, and theological significance
- **The Passion Narratives** – Detailed study of the final week

#### Acts and Early Church
- **Acts: The Continuing Work of Christ** – Spirit-empowered expansion
- **The Apostolic Fathers** – Writings of the generation after the apostles
- **Early Church Life and Practice** – Worship, community, and mission in the first centuries

#### Pauline Epistles
- **Romans: The Masterpiece** – In-depth verse-by-verse study
- **1 Corinthians: Church Problems and Solutions** – Unity, gifts, love, and resurrection
- **2 Corinthians: Strength in Weakness** – Apostolic suffering and ministry
- **Galatians: Freedom in Christ** – Law, grace, and the gospel defended
- **Ephesians: The Church in God's Plan** – Identity, unity, and spiritual warfare
- **Philippians: Joy in All Circumstances** – Partnership, humility, and contentment
- **Colossians: The Supremacy of Christ** – Countering false teaching
- **1 & 2 Thessalonians: Living in Light of His Return** – Eschatology and daily life
- **The Pastoral Epistles** – 1 & 2 Timothy and Titus: church order and faithful ministry
- **Philemon: Reconciliation and Brotherhood** – The gospel applied to relationships
- **The Theology of Paul** – Major themes across the Pauline corpus

#### General Epistles
- **Hebrews: The Superiority of Christ** – Old covenant, new covenant, and perseverance
- **James: Authentic Faith** – Works, wisdom, and the tongue
- **1 Peter: Hope in Suffering** – Identity and perseverance under trial
- **2 Peter and Jude: Guarding the Faith** – False teachers and standing firm
- **1, 2, 3 John: Fellowship and Assurance** – Love, truth, and confidence
- **The Theology of the General Epistles** – Themes and integration

#### Apocalyptic Literature
- **Revelation: Unveiling the Future** – Interpretive approaches and verse-by-verse study
- **Old Testament Apocalyptic** – Daniel, Ezekiel, Zechariah and their New Testament connections
- **Biblical Eschatology** – Comparing millennial views and end-times frameworks

### Bible Study Methods and Tools

- **Inductive Bible Study** – Observation, interpretation, and application
- **Word Studies** – Using concordances, lexicons, and original languages
- **Historical-Cultural Background** – Context that illuminates meaning
- **Literary Analysis** – Genre, structure, and rhetorical devices
- **Theological Interpretation** – Reading Scripture as unified divine revelation
- **Devotional Reading vs. Study** – Balancing head and heart
- **Teaching the Bible to Others** – Preparing and presenting biblical content
- **Introduction to Biblical Languages** – Hebrew and Greek for the non-specialist

## Track Three: Theological Depth

### Systematic Theology

- **Prolegomena** – How we know what we know about God
- **Bibliology** – The doctrine of Scripture: inspiration, authority, inerrancy, sufficiency
- **Theology Proper** – The being and attributes of God
- **Trinitarianism** – One God in three persons: biblical basis and historical development
- **Christology** – The person and work of Christ in depth
- **Pneumatology** – The person and work of the Holy Spirit
- **Angelology** – Angels, demons, and the unseen realm
- **Anthropology** – The doctrine of humanity: image of God, constitution, purpose
- **Hamartiology** – The doctrine of sin: origin, nature, effects, and transmission
- **Soteriology** – The doctrine of salvation: election, calling, regeneration, justification, sanctification, glorification
- **Ecclesiology** – The doctrine of the church: nature, mission, ordinances, government
- **Eschatology** – The doctrine of last things: death, intermediate state, resurrection, judgment, heaven, hell, new creation

### Historical Theology

- **The Early Church (100–500 AD)** – Fathers, councils, and creedal development
- **The Medieval Church (500–1500 AD)** – Monasticism, scholasticism, and pre-Reformation movements
- **The Reformation (1500–1600)** – Luther, Calvin, Zwingli, Anabaptists, and the Catholic response
- **Post-Reformation and Puritanism** – Confessional development and experiential piety
- **Modern Theology (1700–present)** – Enlightenment, liberalism, fundamentalism, and contemporary movements
- **Global Christianity** – The church in Africa, Asia, Latin America, and the Global South

### Apologetics and Worldview

- **Classical Apologetics** – Arguments for God's existence and the truth of Christianity
- **Presuppositional Apologetics** – Worldview foundations and the impossibility of the contrary
- **Evidential Apologetics** – Historical and scientific evidence for the faith
- **The Problem of Evil** – Theodicy and the goodness of God
- **Faith and Science** – Creation, evolution, and the integration of knowledge
- **Christianity and Culture** – Engaging art, media, politics, and society
- **Comparative Worldviews** – Naturalism, postmodernism, Eastern thought, and secularism
- **World Religions** – Islam, Judaism, Hinduism, Buddhism, and new religious movements
- **Cults and Aberrant Movements** – Identifying and responding to false teaching

## Notes on Using This Curriculum

### Approach and Philosophy

This curriculum is designed for the believer who has moved beyond the basics and hungers for more. It assumes familiarity with foundational Christian teaching and seeks to build upon that foundation with greater depth, breadth, and application.

The tracks can be pursued:
- **Sequentially** – Working through one track at a time
- **Simultaneously** – Taking courses from multiple tracks concurrently
- **Selectively** – Focusing on areas of greatest need or interest

### Suggested Pacing

- **Self-Study:** 2–4 topics per month
- **Small Group:** 1–2 topics per month with discussion
- **One-on-One Mentoring:** Customized to the relationship
- **Formal Program:** Structured semester or quarterly format

### Learning Methods

These courses can be pursued through:
- Personal study with recommended books
- Online courses and video teaching
- Church-based classes and seminars
- Small group studies
- Mentoring relationships
- Conferences and retreats
- Seminary or Bible college courses (for credit or audit)
- Podcast and sermon series

### The Goal

The aim is not merely intellectual knowledge but transformative encounter with the living God. Every doctrine learned should lead to deeper worship. Every book of the Bible studied should result in greater love for Christ. Every discipline practiced should produce more of the Spirit's fruit.

"And this is eternal life, that they know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent." – John 17:3

The deepest Christian life is not the one with the most information, but the one with the most intimate, obedient, joy-filled relationship with Jesus Christ.
