# Pastoral and Ministry Leadership Curriculum

A comprehensive program for pastors, church planters, ministry directors, and vocational Christian leaders. This curriculum integrates biblical scholarship, theological depth, leadership development, and practical ministry skills for those called to lead God's people.

---

## Core Division One: Biblical Foundation

### Old Testament Studies

#### Pentateuch
- **Genesis** – Creation, fall, flood, patriarchs, and the foundations of redemptive history
- **Exodus** – Deliverance, covenant, law, and tabernacle
- **Leviticus** – Holiness, sacrifice, and priestly ministry
- **Numbers** – Wilderness journey, faithfulness, and failure
- **Deuteronomy** – Covenant renewal and Moses' pastoral legacy

#### Historical Books
- **Joshua and Judges** – Conquest, compromise, and the cycle of sin
- **Ruth** – Providence, redemption, and the Davidic line
- **1 & 2 Samuel** – Kingship, the heart of David, and leadership lessons
- **1 & 2 Kings** – Divided kingdom, prophetic witness, and the consequences of unfaithfulness
- **1 & 2 Chronicles** – Theological history and the importance of worship
- **Ezra-Nehemiah** – Restoration, reform, and rebuilding community
- **Esther** – Providence and courage in hostile environments

#### Wisdom Literature
- **Job** – Theodicy, suffering, and pastoral care in crisis
- **Psalms** – Worship, lament, praise, and the prayer life of God's people
- **Proverbs** – Wisdom for leadership and daily life
- **Ecclesiastes** – Meaning, vanity, and the fear of God
- **Song of Solomon** – Love, marriage, and human flourishing

#### Prophetic Books
- **Isaiah** – Judgment, comfort, the Servant, and messianic hope
- **Jeremiah** – Faithful ministry in declining times
- **Lamentations** – Grief, suffering, and the faithfulness of God
- **Ezekiel** – Visions, judgment, restoration, and God's glory
- **Daniel** – Faithfulness in exile and apocalyptic hope
- **Minor Prophets Survey** – Twelve voices calling God's people to repentance and hope

#### Old Testament Theology for Preaching
- **Biblical Theology of the Old Testament** – The storyline of Scripture
- **Preaching from the Old Testament** – Hermeneutics for proclamation
- **Christ in the Old Testament** – Typology, prophecy, and christocentric interpretation
- **Old Testament Ethics for Today** – Applying ancient texts to contemporary life

### New Testament Studies

#### The Gospels
- **Matthew** – The King and His kingdom
- **Mark** – The Servant and the way of discipleship
- **Luke** – The Savior for all people
- **John** – The Word made flesh
- **The Life and Teachings of Jesus** – Integrated study across all four Gospels
- **The Parables** – Interpretation and preaching
- **The Sermon on the Mount** – Kingdom ethics and pastoral application

#### Acts and the Early Church
- **Acts** – The Spirit, the church, and the mission
- **The Apostolic Church** – Patterns for contemporary ministry
- **Paul's Missionary Journeys** – Strategy, suffering, and church planting

#### Pauline Epistles
- **Romans** – The gospel systematically explained
- **1 Corinthians** – Addressing church problems with apostolic wisdom
- **2 Corinthians** – Ministry, suffering, and apostolic authority
- **Galatians** – The gospel defended against legalism
- **Ephesians** – The church in God's eternal plan
- **Philippians** – Joy, unity, and partnership in the gospel
- **Colossians** – The supremacy and sufficiency of Christ
- **1 & 2 Thessalonians** – Eschatology and faithful living
- **1 & 2 Timothy** – Pastoral leadership and sound doctrine
- **Titus** – Church order and godly living
- **Philemon** – Reconciliation and the gospel in relationships

#### General Epistles and Revelation
- **Hebrews** – Christ's superiority and pastoral warnings
- **James** – Practical faith and pastoral wisdom
- **1 & 2 Peter** – Suffering, hope, and guarding against false teaching
- **1, 2, 3 John** – Love, truth, and assurance
- **Jude** – Contending for the faith
- **Revelation** – Worship, witness, and the consummation of all things

#### New Testament Theology for Preaching
- **Biblical Theology of the New Testament** – Major themes and their integration
- **Preaching from the Epistles** – Genre-specific hermeneutics
- **Preaching from Narrative** – Gospels and Acts
- **Preaching Apocalyptic Literature** – Daniel and Revelation

### Biblical Languages

- **Hebrew I** – Grammar, vocabulary, and syntax
- **Hebrew II** – Continued grammar and reading
- **Hebrew Exegesis** – Reading and interpreting Hebrew texts for preaching
- **Greek I** – Grammar, vocabulary, and syntax
- **Greek II** – Continued grammar and reading
- **Greek Exegesis** – Reading and interpreting Greek texts for preaching
- **Maintaining Biblical Languages** – Tools and habits for ongoing use

### Hermeneutics and Bible Study Methods

- **Principles of Biblical Interpretation** – Historical, grammatical, literary, and theological interpretation
- **Genre and the Bible** – Narrative, poetry, prophecy, wisdom, epistle, apocalyptic
- **Context and Background** – Historical, cultural, and literary context
- **Application and Contextualization** – Moving from ancient text to contemporary relevance
- **Teaching the Bible** – Methods for various settings and audiences
- **Exegetical Method** – From text to sermon preparation

## Core Division Two: Theological Foundation

### Systematic Theology

- **Prolegomena** – The nature and method of theology
- **Revelation and Scripture** – How God has made Himself known; bibliology
- **The Doctrine of God** – Existence, attributes, Trinity
- **The Doctrine of Christ** – Person and work of Jesus
- **The Doctrine of the Holy Spirit** – Person, work, and gifts
- **The Doctrine of Humanity** – Creation, image of God, constitution
- **The Doctrine of Sin** – Origin, nature, effects, and transmission
- **The Doctrine of Salvation** – Election, atonement, application of redemption
- **The Doctrine of the Church** – Nature, mission, ordinances, government
- **The Doctrine of Last Things** – Death, intermediate state, return of Christ, resurrection, judgment, eternal state

### Historical Theology

- **The Early Church (100–500)** – Fathers, councils, creeds, and controversies
- **The Medieval Church (500–1500)** – East and West, monasticism, scholasticism
- **The Reformation (1500–1650)** – Luther, Calvin, Zwingli, Anabaptists, Catholic response
- **Post-Reformation to Modern (1650–1900)** – Puritanism, Pietism, Enlightenment, revivals
- **Modern and Contemporary Theology (1900–Present)** – Liberalism, neo-orthodoxy, evangelicalism, global Christianity
- **History of Preaching** – Great preachers and movements through the centuries
- **Denominational History and Distinctives** – Understanding your own tradition

### Ethics and Apologetics

- **Christian Ethics** – Foundations, method, and application
- **Pastoral Ethics** – Integrity, boundaries, and professional conduct
- **Bioethics** – Beginning and end of life issues
- **Sexual Ethics** – Biblical sexuality in a confused culture
- **Social Ethics** – Justice, politics, economics, and the common good
- **Apologetics for Pastors** – Defending the faith in preaching and pastoral care
- **World Religions and Cults** – Understanding and engaging other faiths
- **Christianity and Contemporary Culture** – Engaging secularism, pluralism, and postmodernism

### Confessional and Creedal Studies

- **The Apostles' and Nicene Creeds** – Foundation of Christian orthodoxy
- **Major Confessions of Faith** – Westminster, Augsburg, Baptist confessions, etc.
- **Your Denominational Standards** – In-depth study of your tradition's documents
- **Contemporary Statements of Faith** – Lausanne Covenant, Chicago Statement, etc.

## Notes on Using This Curriculum

### Degree Pathways

This curriculum can be adapted for various degree programs:

- **Master of Divinity (M.Div.)** – The standard professional degree for pastoral ministry; typically 90+ credit hours covering most areas
- **Master of Arts in Ministry** – A shorter professional degree; 48-60 credit hours focusing on practical ministry
- **Master of Arts in Theological Studies** – Academic focus with less practical ministry; 48-60 credit hours
- **Doctor of Ministry (D.Min.)** – Advanced professional degree for experienced pastors
- **Certificate Programs** – Non-degree training in specific areas

### Learning Contexts

This material can be engaged through:

- **Residential Seminary** – Full-time, on-campus study
- **Online Seminary** – Distance learning with flexibility
- **Hybrid Programs** – Combination of online and intensive on-campus sessions
- **Church-Based Training** – Local church residencies and apprenticeships
- **Mentored Independent Study** – Self-directed learning with pastoral oversight
- **Continuing Education** – Conferences, workshops, and short courses

### Denominational Adaptation

Most denominations have specific requirements for ordination that may include:

- Denominational history and polity courses
- Specific confessional or doctrinal studies
- Supervised ordination process and examinations
- Mentorship with denominational leadership

### The Heart of Pastoral Preparation

Academic preparation is essential but insufficient. The formation of a pastor requires:

- **Call** – A clear sense of divine summons to ministry
- **Character** – The fruit of the Spirit and personal integrity
- **Competence** – Skills and knowledge for effective ministry
- **Community** – Accountability, encouragement, and partnership

The goal of pastoral education is not merely information but transformation—shepherds shaped by the Chief Shepherd to feed, lead, and protect His flock.

"Pay careful attention to yourselves and to all the flock, in which the Holy Spirit has made you overseers, to care for the church of God, which he obtained with his own blood." – Acts 20:28
